# Bellabeat Fitness Data Analysis — Case Study

## A bit of background
This is my capstone case study for the Google Data Analytics Professional 
Certificate. I took on the role of a junior data analyst working for Bellabeat, 
a wellness tech company that makes health focused smart products for women. 
The goal was to analyse fitness tracker data and use the findings to suggest 
ways Bellabeat could improve their marketing strategy.

## The question I was trying to answer
How do people actually use their smart fitness devices day to day, and what 
could those habits tell us about how Bellabeat should be marketing their app?

## Where the data came from
I used the FitBit Fitness Tracker Dataset from Kaggle, made available by Mobius 
under a CC0 Public Domain licence. It covers 30 FitBit users over a one month 
period in 2016 and includes data on daily activity, steps, sleep, hourly movement 
and weight logging.

Worth noting — the dataset is quite small and fairly old, so I tried to be 
careful about not overstating the findings. That said there were still some 
really interesting patterns worth exploring!

## Tools I used

- **Tableau Public** — for building an interactive dashboard

## Skills Demonstrated

## What I found
A few things that really stood out to me:

- The average user is only walking about 7,600 steps a day — well below the 
  recommended 10,000
- Users are sedentary for a shocking 16+ hours every single day
- Most users are getting around 7 hours of sleep — just short of the 8 hour 
  recommendation
- Evening time around 6pm is actually when users are most active — not the 
  morning as I expected!
- Only about 1 in 5 users consistently meets the daily step goal
- There is a clear link between being more active and sleeping better

## What I recommended
Based on the analysis I put together five marketing recommendations for Bellabeat:

1. Launch a personalised daily step challenge campaign
2. Promote sleep tracking as a core wellness feature rather than an add on
3. Create targeted marketing for four distinct user segments
4. Send smart notifications timed around natural activity peaks
5. Introduce movement reminders to combat the alarming levels of sedentary behaviour
